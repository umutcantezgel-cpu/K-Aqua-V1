The construction sector is responsible for a significant portion of global CO2 emissions. Consequently, pressure is growing on architects and planners to use more sustainable and lower-emission materials. In building services engineering (MEP), the piping systems used play an often underestimated but significant role in a building's life cycle assessment.

## The Carbon Footprint: A Detailed LCA of Plastic vs. Metal

Many planners and architects instinctively assume that traditional metallic pipes (like copper, stainless steel, or galvanized steel) are more "natural" and therefore more environmentally friendly than modern plastics. However, a scientific and holistic Life Cycle Assessment (LCA) according to ISO 14040/14044 paints a completely different picture. When considering the entire life cycle – from raw material extraction through production and the use phase to disposal (cradle-to-grave) – PP-RCT is vastly superior to metals in almost all environmental indicators.

PP-RCT beats metal in 5 key sustainability categories:

### 1. Raw Material Extraction and Primary Energy Demand
Extracting ores for metal production requires gigantic interventions in nature (open-pit mining), massive water consumption, and the use of toxic chemicals. The subsequent processing into copper or steel pipes requires enormous amounts of energy and generates immense CO2 emissions, primarily through blast furnaces and melting processes that require temperatures well over 1,000 °C.

Polypropylene, on the other hand, is a by-product of oil refining. It is extruded at significantly lower temperatures (approx. 200–260 °C), which drastically reduces the primary energy demand in production.

> **Did you know?** Before polypropylene was widely used, gaseous oil wastes such as propylene and ethylene were often simply flared off during gasoline production, as they were considered a mere by-product. Today, exactly these gases are highly refined and used to manufacture PP-RCT. This binds the carbon long-term in the product instead of blowing it into the atmosphere as CO2.

### 3. Logistics and Transport Weight
A plastic pipe made of PP-RCT weighs only about a sixth to an eighth of a comparable steel pipe. When supplying a high-rise project or a large hospital with piping, you need significantly fewer truck trips for PP-RCT systems. Furthermore, the trucks can be loaded more efficiently without exceeding the maximum permitted gross weight. This enormously reduces transport emissions. ### 4. Lifespan, Energy Efficiency, and Maintenance (Use Phase)
The use phase of a building accounts for the largest part of its environmental impact.
* **Longevity**: While metal pipes often have to be expensively replaced after 15-20 years due to corrosion (pitting, rust) – which means renewed massive material and energy expenditure – K-Aqua PP-RCT systems have been proven to remain maintenance-free for over 50 years.
* **Insulation capability**: PP-RCT inherently has a very low thermal conductivity (0.24 W/mK compared to approx. 50 W/mK for steel and 380 W/mK for copper). Especially with our [multilayer composite pipes](/en/produkte/pipes), this significantly minimizes energy losses during the circulation of heating and hot water.
* **Pump electricity**: Because PP-RCT does not incrust (calcify) and does not corrode, the inner pipe wall remains microscopically smooth for decades. The flow resistance does not increase. As a result, circulation pumps do not have to struggle against an increasingly narrowed pipe cross-section, saving thousands of kilowatt-hours of electricity over the lifespan of the building.

### 5. Circular Economy and Recycling (End-of-Life)
At the end of its long life cycle, polypropylene is 100% cleanly recyclable (Recycling Code "5"). The material can be shredded, melted down, and highly efficiently reused to manufacture other, less stressed plastic products (such as car parts, garden furniture, or transport boxes). At K-Aqua, we live the circular economy and consistently return all production waste, start-up lumps, and offcuts into our own recycling loop (regranulate).
